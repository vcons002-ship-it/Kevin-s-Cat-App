Cat cam

Cats never trigger a treat — only people roll — but every sighting is tracked here: when, on which camera, and roughly where in the frame.

The button flashes when a cat is on camera right now — moving or still — and tapping it pulls up the live feed of the camera that saw it. When more than one room has a cat, the feed rotates between them.

A sleeping cat makes no motion, so the app re-checks for a still cat on the schedule set in ⚙ (Always runs the net every frame). Each sighting is tagged with how it was found — motion, still-scan, find, or track fusion.

Follow-mode safeguards

Feeds normally follow the two most recent sightings and hold. These two only matter if overlapping camera views make a cat near a shared edge trip both — leave them at 0 until that actually happens.

Applies immediately — no restart. Confirm swaps filters a one-off; the cooldown breaks two cameras fighting over the same cat. Neither affects a locked feed — locked feeds never reassign.

Live detection

A real-time view of what the detector sees, with boxes drawn around any person (green) or cat (orange) as it recognises them. It streams only while watching and only to browsers with this open, so it costs little — turn 📺 Live off on a slow connection.

🌈 Trail paints where things moved this episode, coloured by recency — blue = older → red = newest — with a route line through the movement and a legend. It tracks all motion (you included), and a big lighting change resets it rather than flooding the screen.

📍 Last keeps a purple, age-labelled box at the cat's newest confirmed position, so “where is she?” stays answered when nothing is detected right now. It hides while a live cat box is on screen and does not expire on a timer.

🐾 Follow points the feed at the room that saw a cat most recently, and ⧉ 2nd adds a second feed showing the room before that. Both hold: further detections in those rooms change nothing, so the feeds don't reshuffle while you watch. They only move when a cat turns up in a third room — then that becomes the main feed and the previous one drops to second.

🎞 Smooth runs a dedicated capture thread so the video plays at the camera's frame rate instead of stuttering at your scan rate (which is gated by detection speed — heavy on a slow CPU / big model). It costs a little extra CPU and reads the camera continuously; leave it off if you only need the occasional frame. Smoothness is still limited by the camera's actual output rate.

        A small vision-language model (moondream) reasons about
        the whole frame to answer “is there a cat?” — a different angle from the box
        detectors, good on hard frames they miss and on decoys they false-fire on. The
        full response is shown because the reasoning is the tool: it
        reveals why it answered (e.g. “the round fuzzy object looks like a cat”).
        moondream’s self-reported confidence is meaningless, so instead the answer is a
        majority vote over N passes (its yes/no wobbles run-to-run) and the
        vote ratio (e.g. 4/5) is the honest confidence — split votes are
        flagged “borderline” for review.
        Paste your API key above (stored locally, never logged); it
        authenticates the one-time weight download and cloud inference. Local
        (Moondream 2) needs a supported GPU (CUDA/Ampere or Apple Silicon — no CPU
        path) and keeps frames on the box; cloud (Moondream 3) is the most
        accurate on hard frames but sends the image off-device. It’s an
        occasional evaluation tool, not an every-frame path.

⚙ Setup & settings — the numbered steps, in order. Cameras save with their Save camera button; everything else saves as you change it.



    

      
1. Cameras (watch one or several — each with its own role & settings)

      

        
        
        
        
        
      

      

      

        
        
        
      

      

        Tick Watch on each camera you want running at once. Every camera
        chooses a role — 🎲 Rolls (a person rolls for a treat) and/or
        🐱 Tracks cats (sightings logged) — and has its own detection settings and
        region of interest. CPU: each watched camera adds roughly one camera's
        load, but the neural net only runs when something moves, so idle cameras are cheap.
        
⟳ Round-robin caps that: only a few cameras detect at a time and
        the app rotates through the rest on an interval, so many cameras cost about as much as a
        few (the trade-off is a slower reaction on whichever cameras are currently resting 💤).
        Mark a camera 👁 always-watch to keep it from ever resting (e.g. the
        treat camera); the camera you're viewing live never rests either.
        Credentials are stored locally in config.yaml in plain text.

2. Speaker(s) (where the chime plays)

      
      

        
        
      

      

      

        Found automatically over WiFi (Google Cast — no account or login needed).
        Pick one or more — Ctrl/Cmd-click (or drag) to select several and the
        treat plays on all of them. This device and the speakers must be on the same
        WiFi network. Names match your Google Home app. Connections are kept open so
        there's no "connecting" chime each time. The first option, "This PC (local
        audio)", plays the sound on this computer's own speakers (handy on a laptop/desktop;
        needs the optional local-audio support from setup) — you can pick it, a Cast device, or both.
        A changed speaker choice applies the next time you press Start.
      

      
      Loops a silent clip every couple of minutes so the Cast receiver never unloads — the only thing that actually stops the chime on a Google Home. Trade-off: it holds the speaker active and briefly yields to (won't stomp on) other audio. Leave off if you use these speakers for music. Applies the next time you press Start.
